## What finance is buying

Data Boar is a **configurable discovery and evidence** platform. It helps locate and classify *possible* personal and sensitive data on **approved targets**. It is **not** a certification, **not** legal advice, and **not** a guarantee that administrative fines go to zero.

## Statutory shape (no hardcoded vendor dollars)

Regulators describe administrative sanctions as **percentages of revenue** (or worldwide turnover) **with a statutory cap** — see **LGPD Art. 52** and **GDPR Art. 83** in the current legal text. Exact bands change; **do not** treat this deck as a calculator.

Industry **cost-of-a-breach** studies (for example the public **IBM Cost of a Data Breach** report) are **external benchmarks**. Cite the **source by name**; do **not** paste a year-locked USD figure here — those numbers age and are not Data Boar’s measurement.

## Shared responsibility (money follows owners)

| Party | Owns |
| ----- | ---- |
| **Data Boar** | Configured scans, structured findings, repeatable artefacts |
| **Customer** | Lawful scope, legal basis, risk acceptance, credentials, retention, **interpretation** of findings |

A clean scan of a **narrow** target set does not prove the organisation is compliant. It proves you produced **technical signals** for that session.

## Questions a CFO should ask in the room

1. Which **systems** are in the first 30/60/90-day scope — and who signs that list?
1. Who consumes the evidence pack (IT, DPO, legal, board) — [DECISION_MAKER_VALUE_BRIEF.md](../DECISION_MAKER_VALUE_BRIEF.md) *Minimum procurement questions*.
1. Which **controls stay outside** the tool (IAM, DLP, incident response retainers)?
1. Open-core vs commercial surface: [LICENSING_OPEN_CORE_AND_COMMERCIAL.md](../LICENSING_OPEN_CORE_AND_COMMERCIAL.md), [SUBSCRIPTION_TIERS.md](../SUBSCRIPTION_TIERS.md) (no public price list in this repo).

## What this is not

- Not a promise to **eliminate fines**.
- Not a substitute for **cyber insurance** underwriting.
- Not a **TCO model** of your entire GRC stack.
